The Padilla Lab is an interdisciplinary lab that utilizes a range of tools to study and advance RNA nanomedicine. A typical Padilla Lab member will spend their time synthesizing novel molecules, formulating nanoparticles, employing advanced characterization methods, performing cellular assays, and testing their creations in vivo — sometimes all in the same week!

If that range excites you, we want to hear from you.

<h2 class="position-title">Quantitative Proteomics and Lipid Nanoparticle Protein Corona</h2>
<p class="position-meta">Co-mentored with <strong>Prof. Rebecca Pinals</strong> (Assistant Professor of Chemical Engineering, Sarafan ChEM-H Institute Scholar), <strong>Prof. Marshall Padilla</strong> (Assistant Professor of Materials Science and Engineering, Sarafan ChEM-H Institute Scholar) and <strong>Dr. Dina Schuster</strong> (Assistant Director of Chemoproteomics, Sarafan ChEM-H Nucleus)</p>
</div>

The Pinals Lab, Padilla Lab, and Chemoproteomics Group are seeking a postdoctoral researcher to initiate an exciting collaboration within the Sarafan ChEM-H Institute at Stanford focused on identifying the mechanisms that govern protein corona assembly on lipid nanoparticles in the context of nanoparticle delivery. This project applies the Pinals lab's corona characterization methods to the Padilla lab's formulation and biophysical techniques, with quantitative proteomics through Dr. Schuster at the ChEM-H Nucleus.

The role is split between this research project and work within the Nucleus, where you will design experiments and actively collaborate with facility users and help develop nanoparticle-corona proteomics into a validated service offering. You will receive insights and training as to how a collaborative research center operates, including method development, validation and QC, instrument maintenance, user support, and the operational side. This opportunity is geared for postdocs who are interested in running a facility, or those who wish to focus their industry or academic careers at the intersection of analytical characterization and therapeutic delivery.

<strong style="color: var(--global-theme-color);">Requirements</strong>

- PhD in analytical chemistry, chemical biology, bioengineering, chemical engineering, or a related field.
- Strong hands-on lab experience in analytical chemistry, biochemistry, or related experimental techniques.
- Proficiency with state-of-the-art Bruker and Thermo Fisher Scientific proteomics platforms.
- Hands-on experience with quantitative nanoflow LC-MS/MS proteomics across sample preparation, data analysis, data interpretation, and refinement for publication.
- Ability to maintain detailed records of experiments and results.
- Ability to manage multiple projects and work under deadlines.
- Excellent organizational and strong communication skills.
- Genuine excitement about working with and teaching other scientists.

<strong style="color: var(--global-theme-color);">Desirable</strong>

- Experience with nanoparticle synthesis and physicochemical characterization, particularly lipid nanoparticles.
- Experience with characterizing nanoparticle-protein interactions, protein corona formation, or colloidal systems.
- Experience with mass spectrometer operation and maintenance.
- Expertise in label-free quantitative proteomics workflows (DIA, DDA) and targeted acquisition strategies (PRM) and the corresponding analysis software (Spectronaut, Spectromine, FragPipe, MaxQuant, Skyline, etc.).
- Ability to work with large datasets using established bioinformatics tools and programming languages (preferred coding language: R).

<strong style="color: var(--global-theme-color);">Appointment and salary.</strong>
Initial one-year appointment, renewable, with funding anticipated for up to three years. Salary follows Stanford's postdoctoral scholar minimum salary scale, set by years of postdoctoral experience ([funding rates and guidelines](https://postdocs.stanford.edu/postdoc-admins/policy/funding-rates-and-guidelines)).

<div class="position-head" id="cryo-em" markdown="0">
<div class="position-label">Postdoctoral Scholar</div>
<h2 class="position-title">Cryo-Electron Tomography and 4D-STEM of Lipid Nanoparticles</h2>
<p class="position-meta">Co-mentored with <strong>Prof. Colin Ophus</strong> (Materials Science &amp; Engineering) and <strong>Prof. Marshall Padilla</strong> (Assistant Professor of Materials Science and Engineering, Sarafan ChEM-H Institute Scholar)</p>
</div>

The Ophus and Padilla Labs have an opening for a co-mentored postdoctoral researcher dedicated to advancing transmission electron microscopy for lipid nanoparticles (LNPs). We are looking for researchers who can:

1. Collect and reconstruct cryogenic-electron tomography (cryoET) and 4D-STEM data on beam-sensitive samples including LNPs.
2. Develop and extend reconstruction and analysis code, including multislice and deep image prior approaches.
3. Formulate and characterize the lipid nanoparticles used in these studies.
4. Correlate the resulting structures against scattering, fractionation and biological efficacy data to build genuine structure–function relationships.

This position is designed for researchers interested in using transmission electron microscopy to study therapeutically relevant soft-matter drug delivery systems. You would build an end-to-end skillset spanning nanoparticle formulation, biophysical and materials characterization, and biological testing — a combination very few microscopists have, and one that travels well into academia, industry or a national laboratory.

<strong style="color: var(--global-theme-color);">Required</strong>

- PhD in physics, materials science, chemistry, electrical engineering or a related field.
- Demonstrated experience in transmission or scanning transmission electron microscopy combined with computational reconstruction — cryoET, 4D-STEM, electron ptychography, or other phase retrieval methods.
- Strong scientific programming, particularly Python.
- Willingness to take on hands-on wet-lab formulation, microscopy characterization and biological testing.

<strong style="color: var(--global-theme-color);">Desirable</strong>

- Cryogenic specimen preparation and low-dose imaging.
- Experience with AreTomo, py4DSTEM, quantEM or comparable toolkits.
- Multislice electron scattering simulation.
- Nanoparticle formulation or drug delivery.
- Small-angle scattering.

<strong style="color: var(--global-theme-color);">Appointment and salary.</strong>
Initial one-year appointment, renewable, with funding anticipated for up to three years. Salary follows Stanford's postdoctoral scholar minimum salary scale, set by years of postdoctoral experience ([funding rates and guidelines](https://postdocs.stanford.edu/postdoc-admins/policy/funding-rates-and-guidelines)).

## Other ways to join

<strong style="color: var(--global-theme-color);">Graduate students.</strong>
Prospective and current PhD students in Materials Science & Engineering and other Stanford PhD programs are welcome to reach out about rotations and projects — tell us what problems you want to work on.

A note on how joining works: students come into the lab through Stanford's admissions, not by direct match. You'll first need to be admitted to a Stanford PhD program, then contact us once you're here — so the strongest first step is applying to the program that best fits your interests. Once admitted, we encourage incoming students to apply to the **[Chemistry/Biology Interface (CBI) Training Program](https://chemh-cbi.stanford.edu/)**, which supports interdisciplinary trainees across chemistry, engineering, and the biosciences.

<strong style="color: var(--global-theme-color);">Fellowship-funded postdocs.</strong>
Beyond the two positions above, we welcome inquiries from exceptional candidates who hold — or are actively applying for — their own fellowship support, such as an NIH F32 or K99/R00, NSF, HHMI Hanna Gray, Damon Runyon, Jane Coffin Childs, EMBO, or an equivalent. If that's you, please email me a short description of the problems you want to work on, your CV, and the fellowship you hold or intend to pursue.

<strong style="color: var(--global-theme-color);">Undergraduate researchers.</strong>
Undergraduates are welcome to contact the lab beginning in 2028.
